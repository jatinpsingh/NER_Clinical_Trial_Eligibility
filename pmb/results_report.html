Overview

Methodology

Fine-tuning followed Li et al. 2022 (the comparative NER study this project's proposal cites as its primary baseline) so the result here is directly measurable against a published reference point, not just an internal number.

Label alignment

Each word's gold BIO tag is assigned to only its first subword piece; continuation subwords and special tokens ([CLS]/[SEP]/padding) are marked -100 and ignored by the loss — the same convention used for decoding predictions back into words at evaluation time.

Evaluation

Two entity-level metrics, matching Li et al. 2022's dual reporting: strict (exact (type, start, end) match) and relaxed (correct type + overlapping span, matched greedily one-to-one per type so no entity is double-counted). Both via chia_pipeline-decoded predictions (BIO tags → character-offset spans via chia_pipeline.align.bio_to_spans, after asserting the retokenized *_spans.jsonl text matches the stored tokens exactly), scored against the same gold spans.

Relaxed scorer — local implementation, pending the shared version The relaxed scorer is owned by a teammate and hadn't landed in the shared chia_pipeline.eval_utils at the time of this run. evaluate.py now ships its own local relaxed_scoring.py implementing the exact contract specified for that teammate's version (verified against the same worked example: type-match + span-overlap, one-to-one greedy matching), used automatically whenever the shared version isn't yet available. Once the teammate's official version lands, evaluate.py will prefer it automatically — these numbers should be spot-checked against it then in case of subtle implementation differences.

Training dynamics

Train loss dropped steadily across all 10 epochs, while validation loss actually climbed after epoch 2 — a textbook overfitting signal on the loss curve. But the entity-level F1 on validation didn't degrade alongside it; it kept slowly improving and peaked at the final epoch. That's why load_best_model_at_end (selecting by validation F1, not loss) kept epoch 10's checkpoint rather than reverting to an earlier one.

In context Every single number here — strict and relaxed, precision/recall/F1, val and test — lands at or slightly above Li et al. 2022's published PubMedBERT numbers on Chia (strict F1 0.656 vs. 0.622; relaxed F1 0.770 vs. 0.744) — a consistent, credible result across both metrics, though not a perfectly apples-to-apples comparison since Li et al. averaged over 10-fold CV and this is one fixed split (see Limitations).
On the Wilcoxon rank-sum test — why it's not included A Wilcoxon rank-sum test needs a distribution of scores on each side to compare — Li et al. computed theirs across their 10 CV folds (10 F1 values per model). This run only has one score (one fixed fold, per the "do not re-split" requirement), and the paper only publishes the mean P/R/F1 per corpus, not its 10 raw per-fold scores. A p-value computed from one number vs. one number isn't statistically meaningful, so rather than report a fabricated significance figure, this is left as a direct point comparison above. A genuine test would require either (a) the paper's raw per-fold scores (not published), or (b) re-running this fine-tuning across all 10 of the shared repo's CV folds to build our own 10-value distribution — feasible, but a substantially larger compute job than the single run done here. Happy to do that if a real significance test is needed.

Discussion

Strong types — Person (F1 0.81 test), Drug (0.77), Condition (0.76) all have both high training-set frequency and relatively unambiguous surface forms (drug names, demographic terms, diagnosis phrases), which lines up with the earlier EDA's entity-count chart where these were among the most frequent training labels.

Weak types — Observation (F1 0.22), Mood (0.24), Reference_point (0.0, only 7 test examples), and Multiplier (0.41) all combine low training support with genuinely ambiguous semantics (e.g. Mood marks hedging language like "may" or "possibly," which overlaps heavily with ordinary sentence structure). This mirrors Li et al.'s own finding that Chia's per-type F1 ranged from 0.015 to 0.808 in their study — wide per-type variance isn't unique to this run, it's a known property of Chia's schema.

Reference_point scoring exactly 0.0 on test (7 gold entities) is a support-size problem, not necessarily a modeling failure — with that few examples, a handful of boundary misses can zero out the score entirely.

One real hiccup worth recording: the VM's pre-installed torchaudio (from the base Deep Learning VM image) was incompatible with the freshly pip-installed transformers/torch stack, causing an import crash the first time training was launched (transformers eagerly imports torchaudio for an unrelated audio-loss module). Fixed by removing the conflicting torchaudio install; training then ran clean end-to-end on the second attempt.

Limitations

  • Single fixed fold, not 10-fold CV — the shared data uses one materialized split (test_fold=0, val_fold=1) of the repo's 10-fold scheme, per the "do not re-split" instruction. No cross-fold variance or Wilcoxon significance testing like Li et al.'s original paper.
  • Relaxed scorer is a local implementation, not yet the teammate's official one — built to the exact contract specified for the shared eval_utils.score_corpus_relaxed, verified against a worked example and a different-type edge case, but should be spot-checked against the official version once it lands in the shared repo, in case of subtle implementation differences.
  • GPU substitution — ran on an L4 instead of the originally-scoped T4 due to real-time capacity exhaustion, not a deliberate methodology choice. Shouldn't affect the model's actual results (same architecture, same hyperparameters), only the infrastructure cost/speed numbers above.
